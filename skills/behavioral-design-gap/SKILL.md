# Behavioral Design Gap Diagnostic

Find where psychology, not technology, is limiting a PLG motion, and produce a prioritized fix plan. Based on *The Behavioral Design Gap in PLG* by Manali Hanamsagar (Small Table Studio).

The core idea: most teams treat PLG as a set of product features (self-serve signup, trials, tours) and optimize surface friction. What's actually missing is the **psychological journey from curiosity → confidence → commitment.** Users understand *what* the product does but don't feel *why* it's for them, *how* to succeed with it, or *whether* it's worth committing to.

The full framework, principles, and examples are in [references/framework.md](references/framework.md). Read it before running the diagnostic.

## Step 1: Gather context

Ask for what you don't already have, in one short message:

- The product, the target user, and the job they hire it for
- The activation flow from signup to first value (screenshots, walkthrough, URL, or description)
- How the free tier, trial, and paywall work, and when upgrade prompts appear
- How the roadmap is decided (user requests, behavioral data, or both)
- Any numbers: signup-to-activation drop-off, trial conversion, upgrade rate, retention

Never invent product details or metrics; mark unknowns.

## Step 2: Check the three critical insights

Assess which of these the team is falling into:

1. **Treating PLG as a product feature, not a behavioral system.** Optimizing friction and flows without designing for curiosity → confidence → commitment.
2. **Optimizing for what users say, not what they do.** A roadmap built from feature requests rather than observed behavior (revealed preference).
3. **Misaligning value perception and pricing.** Paywall before the aha moment, a free tier so generous there's no reason to upgrade, or upgrade prompts that interrupt at moments of frustration instead of success.

## Step 3: Run the 5-question diagnostic

For each question, give findings, red flags spotted, and a fix:

1. **What does the user think is happening?** Mental models and expectations from the first 2 minutes (schema theory). Red flags: >50% signup-to-activation drop-off, random clicking on non-interactive elements, "I thought this was for X" feedback.
2. **What is actually happening in the product?** Actual vs. intended paths, hesitation points (hovering, repeated clicks, backtracking), workarounds like exporting to spreadsheets (revealed preference).
3. **Where is confidence built or lost?** Early wins (ideally under 5 minutes), visible progress, social proof at moments of uncertainty, competence feedback (helpful error messages), predictability (self-efficacy theory).
4. **How do users know they're moving forward?** Micro, milestone, and macro progress. Don't start users at zero: "You're already 30% there" (goal-gradient effect).
5. **Does pricing align with perceived value?** Map the value perception timeline: exploration (don't monetize) → first value (signal value) → confirmation (soft prompts) → habit (strong moment) → expansion (natural upgrade). Let users build something they feel ownership of first (endowment effect).

## Output format

Produce a concise report:

1. **Verdict** (2–3 sentences): where the behavioral journey breaks (curiosity, confidence, or commitment)
2. **Critical insights present**: which of the three mistakes apply, with evidence
3. **5-question diagnostic**: findings, red flags, and one fix per question
4. **Biggest gap**: the single highest-impact problem to solve first
5. **Quick wins for this week**: a perception audit of the first 2 minutes, a confidence check on the first 3 wins, and a review of paywall timing vs. aha moments, each made specific to this product
6. **Behavioral metrics to start tracking** (confidence and progress velocity, not just conversion)
7. **What's unknown** and how to find out

Separate observed facts from hypotheses.
