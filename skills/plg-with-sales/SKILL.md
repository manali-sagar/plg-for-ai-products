# PLG With Sales: Hybrid Motion Diagnostic

Figure out what job sales is actually doing, what the product could handle instead, and where sales handoffs help or hurt. Based on *Building PLG When You Already Have Sales* by Manali Hanamsagar (Small Table Studio).

The core idea: teams "add PLG" by bolting self-serve onto a product built for sales to explain. Sales is often compensating for product gaps: explaining unclear value, customizing for use cases, providing confidence. The question isn't "sales-led or product-led?" It's **"What job is sales actually doing, and what can product handle instead?"**

The full guide, with templates and examples, is in [references/framework.md](references/framework.md). Read it before running the diagnostic.

## Step 1: Gather context

Ask for what you don't already have, in one short message:

- The product, the buyer, and how sales and self-serve currently coexist
- Sales call recordings, transcripts, or notes (ideally 5–10, a mix of won and lost). If none are available, ask the user to describe what reps explain in every call
- The self-serve flow from signup to first value
- Every place users get routed to sales (usage thresholds, "contact sales" buttons, trial endings, chat)
- Any numbers: self-serve activation without sales contact, demo-to-opportunity rate, trial conversion

Never invent call content or metrics; mark unknowns.

## Step 2: Check the three critical mistakes

1. **Treating self-serve as a sales replacement.** The product depends on sales' invisible work: qualifying fit, explaining which features matter, social proof at uncertain moments, role/industry tailoring, objection handling.
2. **Testing PLG in a vacuum.** Building self-serve from generic "best practices" instead of mining sales conversations for aha moments, qualifying questions, and hesitation points.
3. **Optimizing handoffs instead of clarity.** Routing logic that feels like interruption: surprise sales calls after usage thresholds, "contact sales" paywalls when users aren't ready.

## Step 3: Map what sales actually does

If calls or transcripts are available, build the table: **Timestamp | What the rep said/did | Why it mattered | Could product do this?** Explanations that appear in every call are product gaps. Common signals:
- "Let me show you how teams like yours use this" → product doesn't show relevant use cases
- "So what you'd do is…" followed by a multi-step workflow → product doesn't guide the first win
- "This works best for teams who…" → product doesn't help users self-qualify
- The same objection handled repeatedly → product doesn't preempt it

Also capture the **aha moments** (when the prospect leans in), **qualifying questions**, and **hesitation points**.

## Step 4: Build the "self-serve must answer" list

Questions prospects ask, when they ask them, and where the product should answer them (onboarding, feature discovery, contextual prompts). Prioritize the questions that come up earliest; the one asked in the first 5 minutes of every call is usually the biggest self-serve blocker.

## Step 5: Audit every handoff

For each route-to-sales trigger: **Trigger | User intent | Actual experience | Should this exist?** Keep handoffs that match clear intent (demo requests, complex questions, product limitations, team rollouts). Remove or reframe those that feel like bait-and-switch (surprise calls at usage thresholds, "talk to sales to continue" at trial end).

## Output format

Produce a concise report:

1. **Verdict** (2–3 sentences): what job sales is doing that the product should be doing
2. **Critical mistakes present**, with evidence
3. **Top 3 "sales explanation" gaps**, with how often each appears
4. **The one gap to productize first** (the earliest in the journey), with a scoped build: who, how long, what it looks like
5. **Self-serve must-answer list** (top questions, when asked, where product should answer)
6. **Handoff audit table**, with keep / remove / reframe for each trigger
7. **Success metrics**: self-serve activation without sales contact (target a 15–20 point lift), demo-to-opportunity rate (target a 20–30% improvement), and whether sales calls shift to complex questions
8. **Weekly 15-minute diagnostic** set up for this team
9. **What's unknown** and how to find out

Separate observed facts from hypotheses.
