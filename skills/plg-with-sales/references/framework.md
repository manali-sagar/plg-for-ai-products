# Building PLG When You Already Have Sales

A diagnostic guide for companies that need both motions to work together.
By Manali Hanamsagar, Small Table Studio.

## The hybrid motion trap

You're hitting scale walls with pure sales. Leadership decides to "add PLG" to reduce CAC and accelerate growth. You build self-serve signup, free trials, product tours. Instead of complementary motions, you get frustration:

- **Sales** complains that free users waste their time and clutter the pipeline.
- **Product** is frustrated that "good leads" get routed to sales too early.
- **Users** are confused about whether to book a demo or try it themselves.
- **Result:** neither motion performs at full potential.

What's actually happening: you're treating PLG and sales as separate funnels instead of understanding where each creates value. Sales isn't just high-touch selling; it's often compensating for product gaps, explaining unclear value props, customizing for use cases, and providing confidence self-serve can't deliver yet.

Companies that nail hybrid motions don't ask "sales-led or product-led?" They ask: **"What job is sales actually doing, and what can product handle instead?"**

## Critical mistake #1: Treating self-serve as a sales replacement

**What it looks like.** You launch self-serve to "let users try before they buy," but the product was built for sales to explain. Prospects sign up, click around, and leave. Sales gets frustrated with unqualified leads; product sees terrible activation and concludes "users don't get it."

**What's missing.** Sales has been doing invisible work that hasn't been translated into product:
- Qualifying fit before someone wastes time in a complex product
- Explaining which features matter for specific use cases
- Providing social proof and confidence at moments of uncertainty
- Customizing the pitch by role, industry, and company size
- Handling objections the product doesn't address

**Step 1: Map what sales actually does.** Record your next 5 sales calls or pull recent recordings. Build a spreadsheet: *Timestamp | What the rep said/did | Why this mattered | Could product do this?* Explanations that happen in every call are product gaps.

**Step 2: Identify the top 3 "sales explanation" gaps.**
- "Let me show you how competitor users typically use this feature" → product doesn't show relevant use cases
- "So what you'd do is…" plus a multi-step workflow → product doesn't guide users to their first win
- "This works best for teams who…" → product doesn't help users self-qualify
- Handling the same objection repeatedly → product doesn't preempt obvious concerns

**Step 3: Pick ONE to productize.** Start with the gap that appears earliest in the journey.
- If sales always explains who it's for: add a role/use-case selector in the first 60 seconds and show a relevant example instead of a generic empty state (1 designer + 1 engineer, 1 sprint).
- If sales always walks through the first workflow: build a guided "create your first X" flow with real data and a template showing what good looks like (1 PM + 1 engineer, 2 sprints).
- If sales always provides social proof at uncertain moments: add contextual "Companies like yours use this for…" messaging (content + simple conditional display, 1 sprint).

**Measure success.** What % of self-serve users activate without talking to sales, before vs. after? Target a 15–20 point improvement. Bonus: are sales calls now about complex questions rather than basic explanation?

## Critical mistake #2: Testing PLG in a vacuum

**What it looks like.** The product team builds self-serve from PLG "best practices": optimized onboarding, tooltips, help docs. Conversion stays flat because you're guessing instead of using the gold mine in your sales conversations.

Pull 10 recent sales calls (a mix of won and lost). Watch for:
- **The aha moment:** when the prospect leans forward. What were they seeing? What problem clicked? What did the rep say right before?
- **The qualifying questions:** what does sales ask to determine fit? Build these into self-serve as a "choose your path" flow.
- **The hesitation points:** when the prospect goes quiet or skeptical. What concern came up, and how did sales handle it? Address it in product before users hit the same wall.

### The "self-serve must answer" list

| Question prospects ask | When they ask it | Where product should answer it |
|---|---|---|
| "Does this work for my industry?" | Before demo | Onboarding |
| "How long does setup take?" | After seeing complexity | Onboarding |
| "Can I import from my tool?" | During demo | Feature discovery: highlight integrations |
| "What if I need an edge case?" | Before closing | Contextual: "Need custom workflows? Talk to us" |

Pick the three questions that come up earliest. The question that appears in the first 5 minutes of every sales conversation is your biggest self-serve blocker.

## Critical mistake #3: Optimizing handoffs instead of clarity

**What it looks like.** Elaborate routing logic: free users who hit usage thresholds get routed to sales, demo requests trigger SDR outreach, high-intent signals create "hot leads." Users experience it as interruption: a sales call they didn't ask for, or a paywall disguised as "Contact sales" before they're ready to buy.

### Audit every sales handoff point

| Handoff trigger | User intent | Actual experience | Should this exist? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Usage threshold hit | Exploring product | Surprise sales call | No: feels like bait and switch |
| Clicks "Enterprise" features | Researching options | Forced form | Maybe: if you explain *why* sales helps |
| Demo request from homepage | Wants to see it work | Scheduled call | Yes: clear intent |
| Trial ending | Evaluating fit | "Talk to sales to continue" | No: create a clear upgrade path |
| Asks a complex question in chat | Stuck on a specific problem | Routed to sales | Yes: legitimate need |

### Handoff moments that add value
- **User hits a product limitation:** "Need custom workflows? Our team can help you build this."
- **User exploring a complex use case:** "See how a similar company implemented this. Talk to our team."
- **User ready to expand:** "Rolling out to your team? Get personalized onboarding from our team."

**Measure success.** Demo request quality: % of demos that become qualified pipeline. Target a 20–30% improvement over baseline demo-to-opportunity rate. User feedback: "I knew when I needed to talk to someone" vs. "I felt pushed into sales."

## The 15-minute weekly diagnostic

Run every Monday to catch hybrid motion breakdowns early.

1. **What job is sales actually doing? (5 min)** Scan last week's calls: are reps explaining product or solving complex needs?
2. **Where do users self-select into sales? (5 min)** Check demo requests and sales chats: what triggered the user to reach out?
3. **Does product create clarity or dependency? (5 min)** Check self-serve activation: can users reach first value without sales?

When the same explanation shows up in sales calls (Q1), users ask for help with basic tasks (Q2), and self-serve activation is low (Q3), you've found your next product improvement. It takes 15 minutes and surfaces issues that would otherwise take months to appear in quarterly reviews.
