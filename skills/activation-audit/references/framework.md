# Why Users Never Reach Value

A practical framework for auditing onboarding and activation.
By Manali Hanamsagar, Small Table Studio.

## 1. The onboarding illusion

Most teams think onboarding is working because users are doing things: signing up, clicking around, completing a checklist. But activity is not progress.

The illusion happens when:
- Users believe they are moving closer to value
- The product is still waiting on invisible dependencies
- No one corrects the mismatch

This gap doesn't show up in activation metrics. It shows up weeks later as churn, stalled usage, and pricing resistance. By the time you see it, trust is already gone.

The real question is not "Did onboarding work?" It is: **"Did the user actually get closer to the thing they came here to do?"**

## 2. The audit framework: four lenses

### Lens 1: What the user thinks is happening
- What outcome does the user believe they are progressing toward?
- What signals are reinforcing that belief?
- Is progress explicit or implied?

Users sign up because they have a specific pain they want solved soon. Does your product clearly show them how that pain gets resolved? If you don't answer that question, the user will, and they are usually wrong.

### Lens 2: What the product is actually doing
- What inputs or setup steps are still missing?
- What does the system need before value can occur?
- Which of those things are invisible to the user?
- Is the product delivering what marketing promised?

**Example.** A user lands on your site. You promise a beautiful website in under five minutes, exactly what they need. They sign up expecting that outcome, go through onboarding, hit activation, and try to build their site. But the UI is complicated and the "five-minute website" path is unclear. They click around. Time passes. Technically, onboarding worked. Practically, the promise failed. You had five minutes to prove you understood their problem, and you didn't. They leave. That's not lurker churn; it's high-intent users walking away.

### Lens 3: Where trust is built or broken
- Are there moments of silence or ambiguity, where the user waits or is unsure what's happening?
- Does the product explain why nothing is happening, or what the system is doing?
- Do users feel confident that value is inevitable and they're on the right track?

Trust erodes fastest when nothing seems to be happening, often in "cold start" moments: the user lands → tooltips get dismissed → the next step is unclear → value feels far away.

The user thinks: "The product didn't show me what I needed."
The team thinks: "The user didn't do the right action."
**That gap is where trust breaks.**

### Lens 4: Whether pricing and product align
- Is the user being asked to pay before confidence exists?
- Does pricing assume value has already been proven?
- Is monetization framed as a continuation or a gamble?

Misaligned pricing is a tax on uncertainty. **Never tax uncertainty.**

**Example.** I used Splitwise constantly, especially when traveling with friends. Then they capped free transaction uploads. Instead of upgrading, I opened Google Sheets. Pricing should have reinforced usage; instead it cut it off and motivated switching, not upgrading.

## 3. Common failure patterns

These show up across SaaS, AI, marketplaces, and consumer products with delayed time-to-value:
- Progress without confirmation
- Setup work disguised as value
- Silent system states
- Checklists that complete before value is delivered
- Monetization introduced during uncertainty
- Trust placed on outcomes the user hasn't experienced yet

If these exist, your metrics can look fine while your product quietly bleeds users.

## 4. Self-audit checklist

For founders, growth, and product teams. Interrogate your product the way your users experience it.

### A. User belief (what the user thinks is happening)
Ask:
- Why did the user sign up today?
- What outcome do they believe they'll reach first?
- Can they explain that outcome in one sentence?

Pressure test:
- If the product froze right now, could the user explain what they're working toward?
- Is the next step obvious without reading tooltips or docs?
- Does the product clearly define what "progress" looks like?

### B. System reality (what the product is actually waiting on)
Ask:
- What must be true before value can occur?
- What inputs, data, or behavior are still missing?
- Which of these are invisible to the user?

Go deeper: does value depend on enough data, enough usage, enough context, or a background process finishing?

Pressure test:
- If value hasn't happened yet, does the product explain why?
- Does it say "we're waiting on X," or does it just sit there?

### C. Trust moments (where confidence is built or lost)
Ask:
- Where does the user have to wait?
- Where might they wonder if they did something wrong?
- Where does nothing happen after an action?

Zoom in on: the first session, first setup step, first delay, first failure, first "almost there" moment.

Pressure test:
- When nothing happens, does the product explain why?
- Does it reassure the user that value is coming?
- Does it show progress even if value is delayed?

### D. Progress signals (how users know they're moving forward)
Ask:
- What tells the user they're closer to value than five minutes ago?
- Are progress signals tied to outcomes or just actions?

Pressure test:
- Could setup be mistaken for success?
- Do progress indicators complete before value is delivered?
- Does the product celebrate the wrong things?

### E. Monetization timing (when pricing shows up)
Ask:
- When does the product ask the user to pay?
- What confidence does it assume at that moment? Has that confidence been earned?

Pressure test:
- Does pricing feel like a continuation or a gamble?
- Is the paywall blocking the behavior that builds habit?
- Would a skeptical user reasonably say yes here?

### F. Internal alignment (for teams)
Ask internally:
- Are growth metrics masking confusion?
- Are we optimizing activation before value is reliable?
- Are we blaming users for actions we didn't clearly explain?

Pressure test:
- Would product, growth, sales, and support describe activation the same way?
- Do our metrics reflect belief and confidence, or just activity?

## Final gut check

Ask every internal team: **"If I were a first-time user, would I feel confident that this product understands my problem and is actively helping me solve it?"**

If the answer isn't clearly yes, that's where the work is.
